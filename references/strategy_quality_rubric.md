# Strategy Quality Rubric

This file defines what strong and weak strategy output looks like.

## Core Standard

A good strategy answer makes choices. It should not merely describe an idea, praise an idea, or create a generic business plan.

A strong answer must clearly state:

- Who the strategy is for
- Who it is not for
- Where the business will compete
- Where it will not compete
- Why the chosen customer will choose this over alternatives
- What capabilities are required
- What assumptions must be true
- What evidence would confirm or weaken the idea
- What should be tested first

## Rating Scale

Use one of these verdicts:

1. **Strong strategic candidate**
The idea has a clear customer, urgent problem, believable advantage, reachable market, and testable next step.

2. **Promising but unfocused**
The idea has potential, but the customer, offer, channel, or advantage is too broad.

3. **Useful niche, not a full business yet**
The idea may work as a service, feature, content asset, or lead magnet but does not yet justify a standalone business.

4. **Weak as currently framed**
The idea lacks urgency, clear buyer, differentiation, or economic logic.

5. **Bad idea unless materially changed**
The idea has low willingness to pay, weak defensibility, unclear demand, or too much execution burden.

## Quality Gates

A strategy output is weak if it:

- Says "everyone" or "small businesses" without narrowing
- Uses generic advantage language like "better service" or "more personalized"
- Avoids naming competitors or alternatives
- Does not include where-not-to-play choices
- Gives a plan without kill criteria
- Treats a product feature as a strategy
- Confuses market size with demand
- Confuses interest with willingness to pay
- Gives a validation plan that does not involve real buyers

## Strong Strategy Signals

A strong strategy usually includes:

- A specific buyer with a painful trigger
- A narrow beachhead market
- A simple first offer
- A visible advantage
- A reason the buyer cares now
- A repeatable acquisition path
- A clear economic model
- A capability the founder can uniquely deliver
- A specific reason competitors are weak
- A fast test that can prove or disprove demand

## Red Flags

Watch for:

- Founder excitement replacing buyer urgency
- Broad audience language
- Education-heavy sales motion
- "AI" used as the differentiation
- No obvious budget owner
- Long sales cycle for a small deal
- Heavy customization
- Fulfillment dependent on founder heroics
- Weak pricing power
- Buyer likes the idea but does not pay

## Final Recommendation Rules

Use these recommendations:

**Proceed:**
The idea has a clear buyer, strong pain, believable advantage, and a practical next test.

**Narrow:**
The idea is promising but too broad. Recommend a sharper beachhead.

**Test manually:**
Demand is uncertain, but the offer can be validated without building much.

**Park it:**
The idea is not bad, but timing, focus, or opportunity cost makes it a distraction.

**Kill it:**
The idea lacks urgency, buyer clarity, willingness to pay, or defensibility.
