# Strategy Example Bank

This file gives calibration examples for evaluating ideas.

## Example 1: Weak Strategy

**Idea:** An AI tool for small businesses.

**Why it is weak:**
- Customer is too broad
- Problem is undefined
- Buyer urgency is unclear
- "AI" is not a strategy
- No obvious channel or wedge

**Sharper version:**
An AI review-response assistant for multi-location home service companies that need to protect local trust and respond to reviews within 24 hours.

**Why sharper:**
- Specific customer
- Specific pain
- Clear workflow
- Obvious buyer value
- Easier to price and sell

## Example 2: Stronger Strategy

**Idea:** Recurring-spend audit service for businesses.

**Sharper strategy:**
A premium recurring-spend audit for mid-market companies that suspect they are overpaying for vendor services but lack internal expertise to analyze contracts and usage.

**Why it works:**
- Clear buyer pain
- Specific customer size
- Financial ROI
- Domain expertise matters
- Buyer already has budget
- Savings can fund the fee

**Where not to play:**
- Consumers
- Very small businesses with negligible vendor spend
- Enterprises with mature procurement teams and existing expense-management vendors
- Customers only looking for free advice

**How to win:**
Win through insider category knowledge, fast savings identification, and executive-ready recommendations.

## Example 3: Useful Niche, Not Full Business Yet

**Idea:** A newsletter about AI tools.

**Problem:** Too broad and crowded.

**Sharper version:**
A weekly teardown of AI workflows for solo consultants who want to replace manual client research with repeatable agent-assisted systems.

**Why better:**
- Specific audience
- Clear use case
- Higher willingness to pay
- Stronger content angle
- Can become a paid template library or consulting funnel

## Example 4: Bad Unless Changed

**Idea:** A social network for local entrepreneurs.

**Why weak:**
- Marketplace cold-start problem
- No urgent pain
- Hard to differentiate
- Requires high engagement
- Existing alternatives already exist

**Possible pivot:**
A curated monthly founder dinner and private deal-flow list for a single mid-sized metro's business owners doing $1M+ in annual revenue.

**Why pivot is better:**
- Smaller market
- Higher trust
- Easier to start manually
- Clear exclusivity
- Can validate without software

## Example 5: Good Where-Not-to-Play Choice

**Broad idea:** Legacy storytelling platform.

**Strong where-not-to-play:**
- Not for people who want a printed book as the primary product
- Not for professional genealogy research
- Not for users who want complex editing tools
- Not for families unwilling to invite older relatives into voice-based storytelling
- Not for enterprise senior-living sales until consumer demand is clearer

**Why this helps:**
It keeps the product focused on voice-first emotional preservation rather than becoming a bloated memory platform.

## Example 6: Strong How-to-Win Statement

**Weak:**
We win with better customer experience.

**Strong:**
We win by making the emotionally hard task of preserving a parent's stories feel as simple as answering a phone call, while giving the family instant access to the voice, transcript, and living story timeline.

**Why strong:**
- Names the emotional friction
- Names the product mechanism
- Names the family benefit
- Creates contrast against printed or delayed alternatives
