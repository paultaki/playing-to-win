# Execution Patterns

This file provides operator-tested patterns for turning strategy into execution. Use as judgment calibration when the strategy involves rollout, sales motion, organizational adoption, or operating cadence.

## Pattern 1: Pre-Build Privately, Facilitate Publicly

The leader works through the strategy alone first:

- Clarifies the real problem
- Sketches possible plays
- Pressure-tests assumptions
- Builds draft answers to the P2W questions

Then facilitates the team toward ownership rather than presenting the finished answer.

Use when recommending rollout:

- Give the leader a private draft first.
- Then create facilitation questions for the team.
- Let the team discover the strategy inside clear guardrails.

**Principle:** People rarely fight what they help build.

## Pattern 2: Loose Guardrails, Fierce Ownership

Separate high-level direction from ground-level execution.

Leadership owns:

- Winning aspiration
- Where to play
- How to win
- Strategic guardrails
- Metrics and cadence

Teams own:

- Local tactics
- Market adaptation
- Customer-specific execution
- Feedback and improvement

Use when evaluating organizational strategy:

- Do not over-design every tactic.
- Define the few non-negotiables.
- Push tactical ownership closer to the work.

## Pattern 3: The Critical Few

Apply ruthless focus to the small number of actions and metrics that move the business.

Use this as a filter:

- What are the 3 to 5 priorities that actually matter?
- Which metrics are distractions?
- Which activities create motion but not progress?
- What should be removed from the plan?

**Principle:** What you say no to is as important as what you say yes to.

## Pattern 4: War Gaming Before Launch

Use red-team thinking to break strategies before the market does.

Use these five failure dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Market response | What if customers value something different than we think? |
| Competitive countermoves | What will competitors do to neutralize us? |
| Execution breakdowns | Where will process, handoffs, or capabilities fail? |
| Assumption testing | What belief are we treating as fact? |
| Resource constraints | What breaks if time, budget, or talent is reduced? |

**Principle:** Break the strategy in the lab, not in the market.

## Pattern 5: Communication Matrix

Treat communication as execution infrastructure, not announcement theater.

Use three levels:

| Audience | What they need |
|---|---|
| Frontline | What this means for their daily work, success, and pain |
| Middle managers | How to translate the strategy and coach behavior |
| Senior leaders | Strategic logic, tradeoffs, risks, and proof of progress |

**Rule of Seven:** Repeat the message multiple times, in multiple formats, through multiple channels.

**Principle:** Strategy without communication is just a good idea that dies alone.

## Pattern 6: Initiative Tracking

Track initiatives to build evidence, learning, and credibility.

For each initiative, capture:

- Business challenge
- Hypothesis
- Key metrics
- Implementation steps
- Timeline
- Actual results
- Lessons learned
- Repeat, scale, or kill decision

Use this when building validation plans or operating cadences.

**Principle:** Show the causal chain, not just the result.

## Pattern 7: Leading Indicator Dashboards

Prefer visible, simple dashboards tied to behavior.

Examples:

- Prospecting calls
- Meetings attended
- Proposals sent
- Funnel adds
- Funnel health
- Run rate
- Adoption behavior
- Customer response patterns

Use this when defining management systems.

**Principle:** Lagging metrics tell you what happened. Leading indicators tell you what to fix.

## Pattern 8: Behavior Systems Before Outcome Demands

Look for the behavior system behind the outcome.

Ask:

- What behavior are we trying to create?
- What incentive supports that behavior?
- What dashboard makes it visible?
- What recognition reinforces it?
- What manager rhythm keeps it alive?

Use this when analyzing sales motions, transformation work, or go-to-market plans.

## Pattern 9: Proof Before Polish

Value manually validated proof before overbuilding.

Use this when reviewing new ventures:

- Build the manual version.
- Get buyer reactions.
- Track willingness to pay.
- Convert repeated patterns into systems.
- Build software after proof, not before.

## When to Use These Patterns

Use these patterns when the idea involves:

- Sales strategy
- B2B services
- Consulting offers
- Team execution
- Operating rhythm
- Strategic transformation
- Leadership rollout
- Adoption behavior
- Field enablement
- Reputation, trust, or executive persuasion

Use lightly when the idea is:

- A simple consumer product
- A content experiment
- A one-person side project
- A narrow landing page test
