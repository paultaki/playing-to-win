# Positioning Patterns

Use this file to sharpen business ideas into clearer market positions.

## Strong Positioning Pattern

For [specific buyer] who [urgent problem], [offer] helps them [specific outcome] without [specific pain], unlike [alternative], because [unique advantage].

**Example:**
For mid-market companies that suspect they are overpaying for recurring vendor services, the service finds savings opportunities without forcing the team to decode contracts and usage data themselves, because it is built by former category insiders who understand the system from the inside.

## Weak Positioning Patterns

Avoid:
- AI for small businesses
- The future of productivity
- A better way to manage everything
- One platform for all your needs
- Helping people unlock their potential
- We combine AI, automation, and insights
- Trusted solutions for modern businesses

**Why weak:** These do not name the buyer, pain, trigger, or advantage.

## Wedge Strategy Patterns

A good wedge is:
- Narrow
- Painful
- Easy to understand
- Manually testable
- Connected to a larger future market

**Examples:**
- Start with one vendor-spend category before becoming broader expense management
- Start with AI visibility audits before becoming reputation intelligence
- Start with voice-first family stories before becoming a living family archive
- Start with merit aid pages for specific colleges before becoming a full scholarship guidance platform

## Premium Positioning Signals

Premium positioning should emphasize:
- Clarity
- Trust
- Speed
- Expertise
- Reduced risk
- Executive-ready output
- Fewer decisions for the buyer
- High cost of doing nothing

Avoid:
- Over-explaining
- Discount language
- Too many packages
- Too many features
- "Affordable" if selling expertise
- Sounding like a tool when selling judgment

## Where-Not-to-Play Examples

Use these to force sharper choices.

Not for:
- Buyers who need a free tool
- Buyers without urgent pain
- Buyers who require heavy customization
- Buyers outside the clear ICP
- Buyers who need education before they feel pain
- Channels where acquisition cost exceeds likely revenue
- Enterprise buyers before proof exists
- Consumers if the economic model requires B2B pricing

## Offer Sharpening Questions

Ask:
- Who has the pain today?
- Who has budget?
- What event makes them care now?
- What are they already using instead?
- What is the painful workaround?
- What can be sold manually before software exists?
- What proof would make this obvious?
- What should be excluded from version one?
